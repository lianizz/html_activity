Sinigang

Sinigang is a Filipino classic, beloved for its distinctive and mouth-puckering sour flavor. This hearty soup is a comforting medley of tender meat, most often pork, but also beef, shrimp, or fish, and a colorful assortment of fresh vegetables, all simmered in a tangy broth. The signature sourness comes from a souring agent, traditionally tamarind, which gives the soup its unique "asim-kilig," in sinigang a term that roughly translates to a tingling sour sensation. More than just a dish, sinigang is a taste of home for many Filipinos, a warm and satisfying meal that is perfect for a cool, rainy day or any time you crave a savory and invigorating soup, often served steaming hot alongside a bowl of rice.

Comparison of Sinigang to other Recipe

Sinigang's distinctive sour and savory profile sets it apart from other Filipino soups and international counterparts. While it shares a similarity with Vietnamese Canh Chua, which also uses tamarind, sinigang's flavor is defined by its pure, tangy "asim-kilig" and the absence of pineapple's sweetness or the heat often found in the Vietnamese dish. It also differs from Thai Tom Yum, which uses a citrus-based sourness from lime and lemongrass, resulting in a lighter, more aromatic broth compared to sinigang's robust, earthy tamarind base. Other Filipino soups, such as Bulalo and Nilaga, share the use of meat and vegetables but lack the souring agent, focusing on a clear, savory, and rich broth instead. Ultimately, what makes sinigang unique is its foundation in a specific souring agent—most traditionally tamarind—which gives it an irreplaceable identity among the world's sour soups.

Course Type: Sinigang a savory and sour soup often serve with steamed rice

Cuisine: Pork Sinigang is a staple Filipino dish

Keyword:Sinigang

Prep Time: Preparation for Pork Sinigang takes 15 to 20 minutes.

Cooking Time:Pork Sinigang can take 60 to 80 minutes to make from start to finish.

Serving: sinigang pot serves 4 to 6 people.

Calories:A serving of Pork Sinigang contains around 300 to 400 calories.

Ingredients

  • Pork
  • Water
  • Sampalok
  • Tomato
  • Onion
  • Okra
  • labanos
  • Gabby
  • Kangkong
  • Sitaw
  • Pinch pepper to taste
  • Salt
  • Patis

Instructions

  1. Heat oil in a pot over medium heat. Sauté tomato and onion
  2. Add the pork pieces and cook until lightly browned.
  3. Add the seasonings
  4. Add water and bring to a boil. Lower the heat and simmer until pork is tender (about 10-25 minutes).
  5. Add the vegetables and continue cooking until vegetables are soft.
  6. Let it cook for over 10 mins
  7. Serve hot with steamed rice.
